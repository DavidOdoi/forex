## Singapore CFD rules for retail traders

MAS treats CFDs and leveraged foreign exchange (LFX) products as unlisted margined derivatives. It has introduced enhanced requirements for firms offering these products to retail investors, including higher margins, disclosures, and client-money protections. [mas.gov](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/MAS/News-and-Publications/Consultation-Papers/Response-to-Feedback-on-Proposed-Regulatory-Requirements-for-Unlisted-Margined-Derivatives-Offered-to-Retail-Investors.pdf)

For CFDs on FX and other LFX contracts offered to retail investors, MAS set a minimum margin rate of 5% of notional transaction value. This is equivalent to maximum leverage of 20:1 for those products. [mas.gov](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/MAS/News-and-Publications/Consultation-Papers/Response-to-Feedback-on-Proposed-Regulatory-Requirements-for-Unlisted-Margined-Derivatives-Offered-to-Retail-Investors.pdf)

| Product | Minimum retail margin | Maximum retail leverage |
|---|---:|---:|
| CFD on FX | 5% | 20:1 |
| Other LFX contract | 5% | 20:1 |

MAS also requires CMS licensees dealing in LFX, including CFDs on FX, to provide a risk disclosure document before opening a trading account and to obtain a signed acknowledgement from the customer. [mas.gov](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/mas/resource/publications/consult_papers/2012/28-may-2012-review-of-regulatory-framework-for-unlisted-margined-derivatives.pdf)

These protections apply to retail clients. Accredited, expert, and institutional investors may have different rules and protections.

## MAS protections explained

MAS’s retail framework for CFDs and LFX includes several protections:

- **Leverage limits:** A 5% minimum margin applies to CFDs on FX and other LFX contracts offered to retail investors, equivalent to maximum leverage of 20:1. [mas.gov](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/MAS/News-and-Publications/Consultation-Papers/Response-to-Feedback-on-Proposed-Regulatory-Requirements-for-Unlisted-Margined-Derivatives-Offered-to-Retail-Investors.pdf)
- **Risk disclosure:** CMS licensees dealing in LFX must provide a risk disclosure document before opening a trading account and obtain a signed acknowledgement. [mas.gov](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/mas/resource/publications/consult_papers/2012/28-may-2012-review-of-regulatory-framework-for-unlisted-margined-derivatives.pdf)
- **Client-money segregation:** CMS licensees must deposit customer money and assets in segregated trust accounts maintained with a bank, merchant bank, or finance company in Singapore. [mas.gov](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/mas/resource/publications/consult_papers/2012/28-may-2012-review-of-regulatory-framework-for-unlisted-margined-derivatives.pdf)
- **Capital and financial-resource requirements:** CMS licensees must maintain minimum base capital and financial resources above total risk requirements. [mas.gov](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/regulations/securities-and-futures-financial-and-margin-requirements-for-holders-of-capital-markets-services-licences-regulations)

These protections apply to retail clients. Accredited, expert, and institutional investors may have different rules and protections.

## Investor protection: what it covers

Investor-protection arrangements differ by country and entity. An overseas licence does not automatically mean that a Singapore investor is covered by the same protections as a client of a MAS-regulated firm.

Before opening an account, confirm:

- The exact contracting company
- Its MAS status and permissions
- The applicable investor-protection arrangement
- The eligibility rules and compensation limit
- Whether the arrangement covers CFDs and leveraged forex
- Whether trading losses are covered

Investor protection does **not** insure trading losses. It may protect eligible clients where a regulated firm fails and cannot return client assets or meet a legal liability.

### Is negative-balance protection the same as investor protection?

No. Negative-balance protection limits a retail client’s liability to the funds in the CFD trading account. Investor protection may protect eligible clients if a regulated firm fails and owes them a legal liability; it does not insure trading losses.
