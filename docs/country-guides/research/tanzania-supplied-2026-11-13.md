## Forex trading regulations in Tanzania

Tanzania's financial system has two important regulatory authorities:

- The **Bank of Tanzania (BoT)** oversees banking, foreign-exchange operations, authorized foreign-exchange dealers, and other financial activities within its mandate.
- The **Capital Markets and Securities Authority (CMSA)** regulates securities-market participants, including licensed stockbrokers, dealers, and investment advisers.

Ordinary foreign-currency exchange through authorized financial institutions is different from leveraged online forex or CFD trading.

### Bank of Tanzania's foreign-exchange rules

On December 22, 2025, the Bank of Tanzania issued a circular addressed to authorized foreign-exchange dealers.

The circular took effect on January 1, 2026.

It retained a directive prohibiting foreign-exchange trading with international currency brokers that are not licensed in Tanzania.

The circular also required authorized dealers to maintain appropriate customer-identification procedures and comply with relevant foreign-exchange regulations.

**Important distinction:** The circular expressly addresses authorized foreign-exchange dealers. Its implications for a particular individual's offshore retail CFD activity require analysis of the applicable legislation, regulations, product, and transaction. It should not be represented as a definitive ruling on every retail trading scenario.

Nevertheless, it is a significant warning against assuming that any international broker can lawfully provide or receive funds for forex services in Tanzania.

### CMSA and investment services

The CMSA maintains a public register of licensed capital-market participants.

A broker's presence on an international comparison website is not evidence that the company holds a CMSA licence.

Likewise, a company's foreign regulatory authorization should not be presented as equivalent to Tanzanian approval.

### Key regulatory considerations

| Regulatory issue | What Tanzanian traders should verify |
|---|---|
| **BoT requirements** | Whether the service and funding arrangements comply with applicable foreign-exchange rules |
| **CMSA licensing** | Whether the company requires and holds the appropriate securities-market authorization |
| **Foreign regulation** | The actual licence held by the contracting entity |
| **Resident eligibility** | Whether Tanzanian residents are eligible for the specific account |
| **Product permissions** | Whether the company may provide the intended forex or CFD product |
| **Investor protection** | Whether client-money and negative-balance safeguards apply |

## Bank of Tanzania and CMSA protections explained

The Bank of Tanzania and CMSA supervise different areas of Tanzania's financial system.

| Area | What it means |
|---|---|
| **BoT foreign-exchange supervision** | Oversees regulated foreign-exchange activities and authorized dealers |
| **CMSA licensing** | Covers authorized capital-market intermediaries and relevant investment services |
| **Foreign broker regulation** | Applies according to the overseas entity and jurisdiction |
| **Consumer alerts** | Help identify suspicious or unauthorized activities |
| **Client-money safeguards** | Depend on the applicable legal entity and regulatory framework |
| **Complaint procedures** | Depend on the product, company, and supervising authority |

The Bank of Tanzania maintains a financial consumer alert service, while CMSA provides tools for verifying licensed capital-market firms.

Neither a foreign licence nor a broker's global brand establishes automatic coverage under Tanzanian investor-protection arrangements.

## Investor protection: what it covers

Investor protection differs across jurisdictions and account types.

Some regulated brokers may provide negative-balance protection or other safeguards to eligible retail clients.

Other accounts may not receive the same protections.

Before considering a broker, confirm:

1. The exact legal company responsible for the account.
2. Its applicable regulator and licence.
3. Whether the account is available to Tanzanian residents.
4. Whether local regulatory requirements permit the service.
5. How client deposits are held.
6. Whether negative-balance protection applies.
7. Whether a compensation or dispute-resolution scheme covers the account.

Investor protection generally does not reimburse ordinary trading losses.
