## Forex trading regulations in the Philippines

The Philippines has a different regulatory environment from jurisdictions that license retail forex and CFD brokers through a dedicated framework.

The Securities and Exchange Commission regulates securities-market activities and has repeatedly warned about unauthorized platforms offering forex-related investments.

On August 24, 2026, the SEC published an advisory concerning unauthorized solicitation of investments for foreign exchange trading and related investment schemes.

Earlier SEC advisories also named specific trading platforms.

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) supervises aspects of the country's banking and foreign exchange system. However, permission to exchange currencies through an authorized financial institution is different from authorization to operate a leveraged retail forex or CFD brokerage.

### What the rules mean for retail traders

| Regulatory consideration | What traders should understand |
|---|---|
| **SEC authorization** | Verify whether the specific company is authorized to offer the relevant investment products or services |
| **Foreign regulation** | An FCA, ASIC, CySEC, or other foreign licence does not automatically authorize Philippine solicitation |
| **Broker identity** | The legal entity holding the account may differ from the broker's global brand |
| **Investor protection** | Overseas compensation or protection schemes may not cover the account |
| **Payment services** | Support for Philippine payment methods is not proof of trading authorization |
| **Investment risks** | Leverage, margin calls, and counterparty risk can produce substantial losses |

For authoritative information, consult:

- [Philippine Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov.ph/)
- [SEC Investor Advisories](https://www.sec.gov.ph/investor-education-and-information/advisories/)
- [Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas](https://www.bsp.gov.ph/)

These sources should be reviewed before publishing any new broker recommendation.

## Investor protection: what it covers

Investor-protection arrangements differ across regulators and legal entities.

An investor compensation scheme may cover certain eligible claims arising from a regulated firm's failure, but it normally does not reimburse ordinary trading losses.

Before using a financial platform, establish:

1. The exact legal company providing the service.
2. Its regulator and authorization status.
3. Whether Philippine residents are eligible.
4. Whether compensation or client-money protections apply.
5. How clients can make complaints or pursue disputes.

Never assume that a broker's advertised international protections apply to every account.
