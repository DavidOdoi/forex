## Netherlands CFD rules for retail traders

The AFM’s CFD framework follows the ESMA product-intervention measures. It restricts the marketing, distribution, and sale of CFDs to retail clients and requires providers to apply leverage limits based on the underlying asset. [afm](https://www.afm.nl/en/sector/themas/over-het-toezicht-en-beleid-van-de-afm/toezicht-en-beleid/productinterventie)

For major forex pairs, the standard retail margin requirement is 3.33%, equivalent to maximum leverage of 30:1. Minor forex pairs require at least 5% margin, equivalent to maximum leverage of 20:1. 

| Product / underlying | Minimum retail margin | Maximum retail leverage |
|---|---:|---:|
| Major forex pair | 3.33% | 30:1 |
| Minor forex pair | 5% | 20:1 |
| Major stock index | 5% | 20:1 |
| Gold | 5% | 20:1 |
| Other commodity | 10% | 10:1 |
| Shares and other assets | 20% | 5:1 |

The AFM also requires a margin close-out rule at 50% of the total initial margin protection and negative-balance protection for retail CFD clients. Negative-balance protection limits a retail client’s aggregate liability to the funds in the CFD trading account. 

These protections apply to retail clients. Professional clients may access different terms, but they lose some retail protections.

## AFM protections explained

The AFM’s CFD framework includes four major protections:

- **Leverage limits:** Maximum leverage ranges from 30:1 to 2:1 depending on the underlying asset. [afm](https://www.afm.nl/en/sector/themas/over-het-toezicht-en-beleid-van-de-afm/toezicht-en-beleid/productinterventie)
- **Margin close-out rule:** A provider must close a retail client’s position when net equity falls below 50% of the initial margin protection. 
- **Negative-balance protection:** A retail client’s aggregate liability is limited to the funds in the CFD trading account. 
- **Risk warnings:** Providers must give clear risk warnings and explain the risks of leveraged trading. 

These protections apply to retail clients. Professional clients may access different terms, but they lose some retail protections.

## Investor compensation: what it covers

Investor-compensation arrangements differ by country and entity. An EU licence does not automatically mean that a Dutch investor is covered by the same scheme as a client of a locally licensed Dutch firm.

Before opening an account, confirm:

- The exact contracting company
- Its home-state regulator
- The applicable investor-compensation scheme
- The eligibility rules and compensation limit
- Whether the scheme covers CFDs and leveraged forex
- Whether trading losses are covered

Investor compensation does **not** insure trading losses. It may protect eligible clients where a regulated firm fails and cannot return client assets or meet a legal liability.
