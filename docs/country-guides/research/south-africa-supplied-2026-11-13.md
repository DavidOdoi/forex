## South Africa CFD rules for retail traders

The FSCA’s Conduct Standard for Authorised OTC Derivative Providers requires providers to assess a client’s financial situation, objectives, knowledge, and experience before executing an OTC derivatives transaction. It also requires providers to explain the nature, material terms, restrictions, and risks of the transaction so the client can make an informed decision. [fsca.co](https://www.fsca.co.za/_api/cr3ad_noticedocumentses(b24d8fc1-379f-f111-b8dc-70a8a57fc5ff)/cr3ad_document/$value)

The FSCA also requires authorised OTC derivative providers to maintain capital, retained earnings, and reserves proportional to the risks of their South African business, and to hold sufficient capital and liquid net assets funded by equity in South Africa to cover potential general business losses. ## Investor protection: what it covers

Investor-protection arrangements differ by country and entity. An overseas licence does not automatically mean that a South African investor is covered by the same protections as a client of an FSCA-authorised firm.

Before opening an account, confirm:

- The exact contracting company
- Its FSCA status and permissions
- The applicable investor-protection arrangement
- The eligibility rules and compensation limit
- Whether the arrangement covers CFDs and leveraged forex
- Whether trading losses are covered

Investor protection does **not** insure trading losses. It may protect eligible clients where a regulated firm fails and cannot return client assets or meet a legal liability.
