# Sri Lanka country-guide research dossier

Research date and source-access date: **10 November 2026**.

**Publication decision: retain draft status and disable commercial links.** The evidence supports a legal-first informational guide, not a “best brokers” ranking. CBSL’s 2019 warning expressly addresses forex trading, related outward remittances, receipt of profits, and encouragement of those transactions. The current Department of Foreign Exchange FAQ also prohibits Sri Lankan bank-issued cards being used for forex trading. However, a significant 2026 overseas-investment regulation introduces provisions that require qualified interpretation before making an absolute statement about every resident, product, or source of funds. [cbsl.gov](https://www.cbsl.gov.lk/sites/default/files/cbslweb_documents/press/notices/notice_20191021_unauthorized_foreign_exchange_trading_e.pdf)

I could not retrieve the deployed Devin Forex page. Its present content, disclosures, links, and implementation therefore remain uninspected. This dossier is research for populating the template, not an approval of that page.

## A. Legal findings and unresolved issues

### 1. What the CBSL notice actually says

I accessed the full text of the specified notice, rather than relying on search snippets.

Its operative wording includes:

> “Accordingly, any other person in, or resident in Sri Lanka is not permitted to undertake forex trading transactions or remit funds outside Sri Lanka for such transactions and receive income or profit earned from forex trading.”

It separately states:

> “CBSL has not granted any permission for ADs to facilitate forex trading or transactions relating to forex trading on behalf of a person in, or resident in Sri Lanka.”

And:

> “CBSL may take actions against both parties who encourage the public to engage in such forex transactions and persons who engage themselves in such transactions, in terms of FEA.” [cbsl.gov](https://www.cbsl.gov.lk/sites/default/files/cbslweb_documents/press/notices/notice_20191021_unauthorized_foreign_exchange_trading_e.pdf)

Important distinctions:

- “ADs” means authorized dealers; the notice identifies licensed commercial banks acting in that capacity.
- Its scope is “a person in, or resident in Sri Lanka,” not simply “Sri Lankan citizens.”
- It discusses unauthorized online forex platforms and dealers. It does not provide a product-by-product classification of leveraged rolling-spot forex, currency CFDs, equity CFDs, commodity CFDs, futures, or options.
- Its warning about encouraging transactions is directly relevant to affiliate promotions, but its application to Devin Forex requires legal assessment. [cbsl.gov](https://www.cbsl.gov.lk/sites/default/files/cbslweb_documents/press/notices/notice_20191021_unauthorized_foreign_exchange_trading_e.pdf)

The retrieved document does not display a publication date in its body. Its supplied filename indicates October 2019; that is not a substitute for an independently verified publication metadata record.

### 2. More recent guidance corroborates a funding restriction

The DFE’s current-transactions FAQ, displaying “Last Update: 19-10-2026,” states in question 30:

> “EFTCs shall not be used make following payments.  
> a. Dealings in foreign exchange (Forex Trading)”

This section concerns electronic fund transfer cards issued by banks in Sri Lanka. It supports a specific statement about that payment channel—not an inference that some other payment channel makes trading lawful. [dfe](https://www.dfe.lk/web/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=402&Itemid=742&lang=en)

The same FAQ permits documented remittances for legitimate purposes such as imported goods, services, education, and medical expenses. Consequently, “Sri Lanka prohibits all currency exchange or foreign payments” would be inaccurate. [dfe](https://www.dfe.lk/web/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=402&Itemid=742&lang=en)

### 3. Material subsequent regulation: June 2026 overseas-investment rules

I accessed the full text of Gazette No. 2493/36, dated 18 June 2026, effective 20 June 2026.

This regulation:

- Rescinds the 2021 regulations on classes of capital transactions undertaken outside Sri Lanka, subject to saving provisions.
- Preserves the operation of section 22 orders during their applicable periods.
- For investments made by converting Sri Lankan rupees, limits the resident-individual category described in paragraph 3.2.1 to overseas employee share ownership plans or employee share option schemes.
- Separately contains broader language in paragraph 4 concerning overseas assets or investments using existing foreign assets or foreign exchange held in bank accounts. [dfe](https://www.dfe.lk/web/images/rdevelopment/1e053526afc012cd3d06e03a8270234c-2493-36-E.pdf)

The consequential wording in paragraph 4.1 is:

> “Any resident may acquire overseas assets from any non-resident or make overseas investments utilizing any foreign assets or foreign exchange held in a bank account in Sri Lanka or outside Sri Lanka without being subject to paragraph 3 of these Regulations.”

Paragraph 4.2 makes authorized-dealer outward remittances subject to Central Bank directions. Paragraph 10.1 requires compliance with the Act, regulations, orders, directions, and other applicable Sri Lankan laws. [dfe](https://www.dfe.lk/web/images/rdevelopment/1e053526afc012cd3d06e03a8270234c-2493-36-E.pdf)

Research conclusion: this is a genuine subsequent development that must be considered. It does not expressly name retail forex trading or CFDs. I have not established that paragraph 4 authorizes those products, overrides the forex warning, or creates a lawful funding route for them.

It would be equally unsupported to claim:

- “Nothing has changed since 2019”; or
- “The 2026 regulation legalized offshore forex trading.”

### 4. Additional instruments and exceptions remain unresolved

The DFE’s current regulations index lists Gazette No. 2493/38, an order under section 22 dated 18 June 2026. The 2026 overseas-investment regulation expressly preserves applicable section 22 orders. The English PDF retrieval attempted during this research failed; its operative contents were therefore not verified. [dfe](https://www.dfe.lk/web/images/rdevelopment/1e053526afc012cd3d06e03a8270234c-2493-36-E.pdf)

The 2026 regulation also recognizes existing investments under previous general or special permissions, subject to their conditions. That is not evidence that a particular retail trader or broker holds such permission. [dfe](https://www.dfe.lk/web/images/rdevelopment/1e053526afc012cd3d06e03a8270234c-2493-36-E.pdf)

Accordingly, legal review must address:

- The current section 22 order and implementing directions.
- Whether leveraged forex and each category of CFD fall within paragraph 4.
- Existing individual or institutional special permissions.
- Residents using existing foreign-currency balances or overseas assets.
- Non-nationals, emigrants, temporary visitors, and residents outside Sri Lanka.
- The meaning and reach of “in, or resident in Sri Lanka.”

### 5. Residency is not simply nationality

DFE’s public residency summary includes Sri Lankan citizens studying overseas, certain temporarily absent citizens, foreigners on resident/employment visas, and incorporated entities among its resident categories. The 2026 regulation refers to the section 31 residency order in Gazette No. 2213/40 of 3 February 2021, as amended. [dfe](https://www.dfe.lk/web/images/rdevelopment/1e053526afc012cd3d06e03a8270234c-2493-36-E.pdf)

The underlying residency order was not accessed in this research. Do not determine someone’s legal residency merely from a passport, IP address, or broker country dropdown.

### 6. Offshore acceptance does not establish local permission

The Seychelles FSA expressly explains that a securities dealer seeking to operate or offer services in another jurisdiction must hold the required exemption, cross-border agreement, or relevant approval from that jurisdiction’s regulator. Its communiqué also distinguishes regulatory enforcement from recovery of client funds. [fsaseychelles](https://fsaseychelles.sc/media/regupdates/Communiqu_No._1_of_2021_-_Awareness_of_Securities_Dealers_Businesses_copy_7.pdf)

For this guide, maintain four separate fields:

| Question | Research status |
|---|---|
| Will this broker explicitly accept this resident category? | Not established for the four candidates from the accessed primary sources. |
| Which exact entity would contract with that resident? | Not established. |
| Does that exact entity have relevant Sri Lankan authorization? | Not established. |
| Are the resident’s trading and funding transactions permitted? | Restrictions are documented; full current product-specific interpretation remains unresolved. |

A foreign licence, website accessibility, or a successful registration is not a substitute for these answers.
