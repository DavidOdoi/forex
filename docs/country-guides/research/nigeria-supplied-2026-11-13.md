## Forex trading regulations in Nigeria

Forex trading in Nigeria operates within a regulatory environment involving the **Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)** and the **Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN)**.

The two regulators have different responsibilities.

The SEC supervises capital-market activities and has statutory authority over online forex trading platforms and intermediaries under the Investments and Securities Act 2025.

The CBN oversees Nigeria's banking and foreign-exchange framework, including the authorized foreign-exchange market and relevant financial institutions.

Ordinary currency exchange through authorized banks or dealers is not the same as leveraged retail forex or CFD trading through an online platform.

### What does the Investments and Securities Act 2025 say?

The Act expressly includes online forex trading activities, platforms, and intermediaries within the SEC's regulatory responsibilities.

On May 8, 2026, the SEC issued a public notice reminding investors that only entities registered with the Commission may promote investment services, provide investment advisory services, or solicit public investment funds in the Nigerian capital market.

This makes regulatory verification especially important when considering international forex and CFD providers.

A broker's licence from an overseas regulator does not automatically establish Nigerian SEC registration.

### Key regulatory checks

| Regulatory consideration | What Nigerian traders should verify |
|---|---|
| **SEC registration** | Whether the exact company is registered and authorized for its activities in Nigeria |
| **Foreign regulation** | Whether the actual account-holding entity has a valid overseas licence |
| **Resident eligibility** | Whether Nigerian residents can open the specific account |
| **Product permissions** | Whether the broker may offer the forex or CFD products concerned |
| **Client-money protection** | How deposits are held and what safeguards apply |
| **Investor compensation** | Whether an applicable protection scheme covers Nigerian clients |
| **Payment compliance** | Whether funding and withdrawals use permitted channels |

Check the [SEC's official registered-operator portal](https://www.sec.gov.ng/for-investors/find-a-registered-operator/) before dealing with a provider.

A broker's international popularity should never substitute for this verification.

## Nigerian SEC protections explained

The Nigerian SEC's regulatory framework is intended to provide oversight of authorized capital-market operators and investment services.

The Investments and Securities Act 2025 expressly recognizes the SEC's authority concerning online forex trading platforms and intermediaries.

| Protection or regulatory check | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Operator registration** | Enables verification of firms authorized for particular regulated activities |
| **Regulatory oversight** | Supports supervision of registered capital-market participants |
| **Disclosure requirements** | Help investors understand relevant products and risks |
| **Complaint channels** | Provide formal routes for eligible regulatory complaints |
| **Enforcement notices** | Warn the public about unauthorized or potentially fraudulent activity |
| **Investor education** | Helps customers assess investment risks and misleading claims |

The SEC's registration requirements are not automatically satisfied by a foreign FCA, ASIC, CySEC, or other overseas licence.

In January 2026, the SEC issued a warning concerning ModMount Services Limited, a purported offshore CFD broker. The notice emphasized the risks of dealing with unregistered investment providers soliciting Nigerians.

These concerns are relevant when evaluating any offshore trading platform.

## Investor protection: what it covers

Investor-protection arrangements differ according to the legal entity and regulator responsible for the account.

Some jurisdictions have compensation arrangements covering eligible customers if a regulated firm fails and cannot meet its obligations.

However, those schemes generally do not insure ordinary investment losses.

Before opening an account, confirm:

1. The exact legal company providing the trading account.
2. Its regulatory registration and permitted activities.
3. Whether Nigerian residents are eligible.
4. How client money is held.
5. Whether negative-balance protection applies.
6. Whether any compensation arrangement covers the account.
7. How disputes and complaints are handled.

Never assume that all companies within a global brokerage group provide identical protections.
