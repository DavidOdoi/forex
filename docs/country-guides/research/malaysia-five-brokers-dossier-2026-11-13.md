A. Resident Eligibility & Entity Matrix

Under Malaysian law, retail leveraged FX/CFD trading is not authorized domestically: the Securities Commission Malaysia (SC) restricts OTC CFDs exclusively to sophisticated investors via holders of a Capital Markets Services Licence (CMSL) for dealing in derivatives (or restricted to CFD), while Labuan FSA money-broking licensees cannot deal in Ringgit or with non-authorized Malaysian residents without domestic regulatory clearance. None of the five candidate brokers holds an SC CMSL for derivatives/CFDs or an active Labuan FSA money-broking licence; instead, ordinary Malaysian residents who sign up online are technically onboarded via offshore entities outside Malaysian investor-protection laws.

2. Client-Money Segregation, Negative Balance Protection (NBP) & Compensation

• Malaysian Statutory Compensation Exclusion: Because none of the five entities holds an SC CMSL, Malaysian residents have no access to the Capital Market Compensation Fund (CMC) or SIDREC (Securities Industry Dispute Resolution Center). Furthermore, even for SC-licensed CFD providers, CMC recourse applies only to default/insolvency of an SC-licensed intermediary and never insures trading losses.
• Entity-Specific Protections:
	• IC Markets (Raw Trading Ltd): Segregated client accounts at tier-1 banks. No statutory compensation scheme. NBP is NOT guaranteed: IC Markets' Seychelles Help Centre explicitly warns that in thin liquidity/gapping events where losses exceed account balance, "you will bear the negative consequences of such adverse events".
	• OANDA (OANDA Global Markets Ltd): Segregated client bank accounts. No statutory compensation scheme. No Negative Balance Protection: OGM does not provide NBP; clients are liable for negative equity deficits.
	• XM (XM Global Limited): Segregated client funds. No statutory compensation scheme. Negative Balance Protection provided by contract (XM Global Limited resets negative balances to zero).
	• FBS (FBS Markets Inc.): Segregated client accounts. No statutory compensation scheme. Negative Balance Protection provided per FBS trading conditions.
	• Tickmill (Tickmill Ltd): Segregated client accounts at tier-1 banks. No statutory compensation scheme for Tickmill Ltd (Seychelles). NBP terms for Tickmill Ltd (Seychelles) vs. UK/EU entities are partially conflated in public summaries (see Manual Follow-Up).

D. Country-Specific Rule Updates (Malaysia)

1. Securities Commission Malaysia (SC) — CFD Framework (SC-GL/3-2018 (R3-2024), revised 14 June 2024; FAQs revised 14 October 2024):
	• Licensing & Investor Restriction: Under CMSA s.377 and Para 4.01–4.02, OTC CFDs in Malaysia may only be offered by a CMSL holder for dealing in derivatives (or dealing in derivatives restricted to CFD) and exclusively to sophisticated investors (as defined in the Guidelines on Categories of Sophisticated Investors). Retail investors are prohibited from being offered CFDs domestically.
	• Permitted Underlying Assets (Para 3.01–3.04A): Qualifying shares, REIT units, and non-leveraged/non-inverse ETF units listed on Bursa Malaysia Main Board or an IOSCO MMoU exchange; listed commodity derivatives on Bursa Derivatives or a Specified Exchange; and qualifying broadly based indices.
	• Domestic Margin Floors (Para 4.12): 5% (indices), 10% (index shares/REITs), 15% (commodity derivatives), 20% (non-index shares/REITs and ETFs).
	• SC & Labuan FSA MoU: SC and Labuan FSA maintain a regulatory cooperation MoU; holding a Labuan licence does not grant SC CMSL permission to offer retail or domestic CFDs in mainland Malaysia.
2. Labuan Financial Services Authority (Labuan FSA) — Revised Money Broking Guidelines (9 September 2024):
	• Under Guidelines on the Establishment of Money Broking Business in Labuan IBFC (effective 9 Sept 2024), Labuan money brokers act strictly as intermediaries (prohibited from acting as principal/market maker), must maintain minimum unimpaired paid-up capital of RM1,000,000 (or RM1,500,000 if including digital assets), and are subject to a 100:1 maximum leverage cap (1:1 for digital assets).
	• Labuan money brokers may transact only in foreign currency (no Ringgit) and require prior domestic regulatory approval or legal confirmation before onboarding Malaysian residents.
3. Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) — Foreign Exchange Policy (FEP) Notices (effective 1 October 2025, revoking 15 November 2024 Notices):
	• Under Notice 3 (Investment in Foreign Currency Asset), a Malaysian resident without domestic ringgit borrowing (DRB) may invest any amount in foreign currency assets onshore or abroad.
	• A Malaysian resident individual with domestic ringgit borrowing is capped at RM1 million equivalent per calendar year in aggregate for foreign currency asset investments funded via Ringgit conversion, Trade FCA, or swapping Ringgit financial assets (RM50 million equivalent per calendar year on a corporate group basis for resident entities with DRB), though investments funded from foreign currency sourced outside Malaysia (excluding export proceeds) are uncapped.
	• Under SC CFD FAQ 2.10, domestic CFD providers must also comply with BNM FEP Notices depending on investor residency and contract currency denomination.
