# AAFX Trading Review 2026: Is It Safe or a Scam? Regulation, Fees & Withdrawal Complaints

**AAFX Trading is an offshore forex and CFD broker that advertises high leverage, multiple trading accounts, and access to international financial markets. However, its regulatory history raises serious concerns about trader protection.**

Operating through the website aafxtrading.com, the broker identifies AAFX Trading Company Limited, associated with Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, as its operating company.

AAFX advertises forex, stock CFDs, indices, commodities, precious metals, energy products, and cryptocurrency-related trading.

Its published trading conditions include leverage as high as 1:2000, a $100 minimum deposit on selected accounts, and several pricing structures.

These features may appeal to traders seeking highly leveraged forex accounts.

However, AAFX Trading differs significantly from established, licensed brokers.

The company appears on the **US Commodity Futures Trading Commission's Registration Deficient (RED) List** and has received warnings from financial authorities in Hong Kong and Spain.

Furthermore, Saint Vincent and the Grenadines does not license forex brokerage activities, meaning local company registration should not be presented as proof of financial regulatory supervision.

This AAFX Trading review examines its regulation, trading conditions, platforms, customer complaints, withdrawal risks, and safer alternatives.

**Our verdict: AAFX Trading is a high-risk broker that we do not recommend.** Its regulatory warning history and lack of a verified forex brokerage licence outweigh the appeal of its advertised leverage and trading conditions.

**Risk warning:** Trading through a broker without verified financial-services authorization may expose customers to significant financial, custody, and dispute-resolution risks. Leveraged forex and CFDs also carry substantial market risk.

## Is AAFX Trading legitimate and regulated?

This is the most important question in the review.

AAFX Trading's website and third-party directories associate the business with Saint Vincent and the Grenadines.

However, **registering a company is not the same as obtaining authorization to provide regulated forex brokerage services**.

The Saint Vincent and the Grenadines Financial Services Authority has published guidance explaining that forex brokerage activities are not licensed in that jurisdiction.

As a result, a business can have a legitimate company registration without being supervised as a licensed forex dealer.

### 1. US CFTC RED List warning

AAFX Trading appears on the [official CFTC RED List](https://www.cftc.gov/REDlist/node/220641).

The regulator identifies:

- Broker: AAFX Trading
- Website: aafxtrading.com
- Operating company: AAFX Trading Company Limited
- Origin: Saint Vincent and the Grenadines
- Listed date: April 25, 2017

The CFTC states that its review identified activity apparently requiring registration while the company was not registered with the agency.

**Important distinction:** A RED List entry is a regulatory warning, not a final court finding of fraud or a confirmed violation of US commodities law.

Nevertheless, it is a significant warning for customers evaluating the broker's regulatory standing.

### 2. Hong Kong SFC warning

The Hong Kong Securities and Futures Commission added AAFX Trading Capital / AAFX Trading Company Ltd to its list of unlicensed entities on March 29, 2017.

The [official SFC alert](https://www.sfc.hk/en/alert-list/1853) identifies several addresses associated with the company.

The regulator also states that the firm supplied a Hong Kong address where it was not actually located.

This raises concerns about the reliability of the company's public contact information.

### 3. Spanish CNMV warning

Spain's National Securities Market Commission issued a warning on November 4, 2019.

The regulator identified AAFX Trading Company Ltd and aafxtrading.com as unauthorized to provide specified investment and auxiliary services in Spain.

The [official CNMV warning](https://www.cnmv.es/portal/resultadobusqueda?ent=2&lang=es&page=1&sortDirection=0&sortExpression=Companias&tipo=1) remains available in the regulator's records.

The warning concerns authorization under Spanish law; it should not be misrepresented as a worldwide criminal judgment.

### 4. Saint Vincent and the Grenadines registration

AAFX Trading is associated with Saint Vincent and the Grenadines.

However, the country's Financial Services Authority has made clear that it does not license forex brokerage services.

Its [official notice on unlicensed forex and binary-options operations](https://svgfsa.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/UNLICENSED-FOREX.pdf) explains this distinction.

A company registration in Saint Vincent and the Grenadines therefore does not establish:

- Forex-dealer regulatory supervision
- Mandatory segregation under a verified forex regulatory regime
- Statutory negative-balance protection
- A recognized investor-compensation scheme
- A regulator empowered to resolve individual forex brokerage disputes

**Our regulatory assessment:** We could not establish a valid financial-services licence authorizing AAFX Trading's forex brokerage activities from the sources reviewed. Combined with the official warnings, this makes the broker unsuitable for our recommended broker lists.

## AAFX Trading leverage: Is 1:2000 safe?

AAFX advertises maximum leverage as high as 1:2000 on selected accounts.

This means a relatively small margin deposit can control a much larger notional trading position.

For example, a $100,000 position at 1:2000 leverage would theoretically require only $50 in initial margin, before other broker-specific requirements.

However, **high leverage significantly increases liquidation risk**.

A relatively small unfavorable price movement can exhaust a trader's available margin.

Furthermore, customers using a broker without verified regulatory oversight may have limited recourse if they dispute trade execution, margin calculations, or account restrictions.

We do not recommend selecting AAFX because of its advertised leverage.
