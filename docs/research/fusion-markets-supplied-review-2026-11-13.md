## Is Fusion Markets a good broker?

Fusion Markets is particularly appealing to traders who already understand forex pricing and want to minimize transaction costs.

The broker's Zero Account allows users to access raw spreads while paying a separate commission. Its Classic Account incorporates dealing costs into the spread, which makes calculating expenses simpler.

An experienced trader using MetaTrader or cTrader may find this combination attractive.

However, Fusion Markets is less compelling for investors who want direct ownership of stocks and ETFs, extensive fundamental research, or the investor protections associated with certain larger brokerage groups.

**Bottom line:** Fusion Markets is strongest as a low-cost forex and CFD trading provider, not an all-purpose investment platform.

## Fusion Markets fees and spreads

Trading costs are the broker's most important selling point.

Fusion Markets offers two principal pricing models: Zero and Classic.

### Zero Account: Raw spreads plus commission

The Zero Account advertises raw spreads starting from 0.0 pips.

For USD-denominated accounts, the published starting commission is:

- $2.25 per side per standard lot
- $4.50 for opening and closing one standard lot
- A proportionally smaller commission for smaller trade sizes

These charges are separate from the spread.

The advertised minimum spread of 0.0 pips does not mean spreads remain at zero throughout the trading day.

### Classic Account: Commission-free trading

The Classic Account does not charge a separate trading commission.

Instead, the broker incorporates its trading charge into the quoted spread.

Its advertised spreads start at approximately 0.9 pips.

This pricing model may appeal to traders who prefer a simpler cost structure.

### Zero vs Classic: Which account is cheaper?

| Feature | Zero Account | Classic Account |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum deposit | $0 | $0 |
| Spread type | Raw, variable | Wider, variable |
| Advertised starting spread | 0.0 pips | 0.9 pips |
| Separate trading commission | Yes | No |
| Best suited to | Active, cost-conscious traders | Traders preferring simple pricing |

For a standard EUR/USD trade, a 0.1-pip spread on one standard lot represents approximately $1 in spread cost, assuming a USD-quoted account.

If the Zero Account charges $4.50 round-turn commission, the combined cost would be approximately $5.50.

A 0.9-pip spread on the Classic Account would represent approximately $9 for the same position size.

These are illustrations, not live quotes. Real spreads and costs vary with market conditions, trade size, and account currency.

## Is Fusion Markets safe and regulated?

Fusion Markets operates through different legal entities in multiple jurisdictions.

This matters because the licence, investor protections, and available trading conditions depend on the company that actually provides the account.

### Fusion Markets regulation

| Jurisdiction | Entity | Regulatory authority |
|---|---|---|
| Australia | Australian Fusion Markets entities, depending on applicable disclosures | Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC) |
| Seychelles | Fusion Markets International Ltd | Financial Services Authority (FSA), licence SD096 |
| Vanuatu | Gleneagle Securities Pty Limited, trading as Fusion Markets | Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC), company number 40256 |

Fusion Markets' current official regulatory disclosures identify the Australian entity serving Australian clients as **FMGP Trading Group Pty Ltd**, operating under Australian Financial Services Licence 385620.

Some older Australian Fusion Markets documents identify Gleneagle Asset Management Limited under AFSL 226199. Readers should rely on the current client agreement and regulator register rather than assuming historical documents apply to their account.

### Does ASIC regulation protect every Fusion Markets customer?

No.

ASIC authorization is relevant to the Australian legal entity and customers covered by that entity's regulatory framework.

International customers may instead contract with companies regulated in Seychelles or Vanuatu.

An ASIC licence held by an Australian entity does not automatically give every international customer Australian investor protections.

### Are client funds segregated?

Fusion Markets states that client funds are deposited into segregated client trust accounts.

This is a useful safeguard, but segregation does not eliminate trading losses or guarantee reimbursement if the broker experiences financial problems.

The precise arrangements should be checked in the applicable client agreement.

### Does Fusion Markets offer negative-balance protection?

Eligible customers under its Seychelles and Vanuatu entities can receive negative-balance protection, subject to policy conditions and exclusions.

The broker explicitly excludes residents of certain jurisdictions, including Indonesia, Vietnam, Hong Kong, Taiwan, Mongolia, Türkiye, and mainland China.

For excluded customers, a negative account balance may remain their responsibility.

**Our safety assessment:** Fusion Markets has identifiable regulated entities and published legal documentation, but the protections are not uniform. Check the exact account-holding company and country-specific terms before trading.
