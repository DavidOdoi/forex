## XTB fees and spreads

XTB primarily uses spread-based pricing for retail forex and CFD trading.

Its Standard Account generally does not charge a separate forex dealing commission.

Instead, the trading cost is incorporated into the bid-ask spread.

### XTB EUR/USD spreads

According to [ForexBrokers.com's October 2026 review](https://www.forexbrokers.com/reviews/xtb), XTB reported an average EUR/USD spread of approximately **1.06 pips in Q2 2026** for retail trades of up to 15 lots.

For larger trades, the reported average increased to approximately 1.29 pips.

These figures are derived from the broker's published group data, not independent live-price measurements by DEVIN FOREX.

Spreads may widen during volatile or less-liquid market conditions.

### Does XTB charge forex commissions?

XTB's Standard forex account generally charges no separate forex trading commission.

The broker earns its trading fee through the quoted spread.

Unlike Fusion Markets or FP Markets, XTB does not offer a conventional raw-spread forex account with a separate commission in its reviewed retail lineup.

### Does XTB charge an inactivity fee?

Yes.

According to [XTB's official inactivity and commission policy](https://www.xtb.com/en/help-center/fees-and-payments-3/fees-and-commissions-at-xtb), a fee of €10 per month, or its account-currency equivalent, applies when both conditions are met:

1. No position has been opened or closed during the previous 365 days.
2. No deposit has been made during the previous 90 days.

The fee is not generally applied when the account has no available funds, and certain exceptions apply to eligible accounts with open positions.

### Does XTB charge currency-conversion fees?

Yes.

XTB generally applies a 0.5% currency-conversion margin when an eligible transaction requires conversion between currencies.

Its [official conversion policy](https://www.xtb.com/en/help-center/fees-and-payments-3/fees-and-commissions-at-xtb) also identifies separate internal currency-transfer charges of 0.5% on weekdays and 0.8% on weekends and holidays.

These charges can matter for investors purchasing international shares in a different currency from their account balance.
