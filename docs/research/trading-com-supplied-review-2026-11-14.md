# Trading.com Review 2026: Fees, Spreads, Safety, Platforms & Pros and Cons

**Trading.com is an online forex and CFD broker known for its beginner-friendly trading platforms, commission-free pricing, and regulation across several major financial jurisdictions.**

Launched as a trading brand in 2019, Trading.com operates through regulated companies in the United States, United Kingdom, European Union, and Australia.

Its biggest attractions include low minimum deposits, straightforward account structures, proprietary web and mobile trading platforms, and support for MetaTrader 5 (MT5).

Unlike many international CFD brokers, Trading.com also offers a dedicated US retail forex service regulated by the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) and National Futures Association (NFA).

However, Trading.com isn't the cheapest forex broker for every trader. It does not offer a conventional raw-spread account, its standard forex spreads can be wider than those of low-cost competitors, and the products available in the US differ considerably from those offered internationally.

According to [FXEmpire's independent March 31, 2026 review](https://www.fxempire.com/brokers/tradingcom), Trading.com received an overall rating of **4.4 out of 5**. Its separately tested US business received 4.2 out of 5 in July 2026.

But is Trading.com safe? Are its spreads competitive? And is it better than OANDA, FOREX.com, or AvaTrade?

This **Trading.com review for 2026** examines its regulation, trading costs, account types, platforms, minimum deposits, withdrawals, customer feedback, and overall suitability.

**Our verdict:** Trading.com is a competitive broker to research for beginners and casual traders who value simple software, transparent spread-based pricing, and identifiable regulatory oversight. Its limitations include the absence of raw-spread accounts, restricted US product availability, and inactivity fees after prolonged account dormancy.

**Risk warning:** Forex and CFDs carry substantial financial risk. Leveraged trading can magnify losses, and customer protections depend on the legal company responsible for the account.

### Are client funds segregated?

Trading.com states that eligible client funds are held separately from its operational funds according to the applicable regulatory rules.

This is an important safeguard but does not prevent ordinary trading losses.

### Does Trading.com offer negative-balance protection?

Eligible UK, EU, and Australian retail CFD customers receive protections under their applicable regulatory and account conditions.

The US forex offering operates under a different framework.

For example, the [US T1 account page](https://www.trading.com/us/t1-account) explicitly warns that forex trading losses can exceed deposits.

Therefore, negative-balance protection should **not** be described as universally available across Trading.com.

### Does Trading.com offer investor compensation?

Eligible customers of the UK and EU entities may qualify for statutory investor-compensation arrangements, subject to the relevant rules.

Those protections do not automatically apply to customers of the US or Australian entities.

Compensation schemes do not normally cover losses resulting from unsuccessful trades.

**Our safety assessment:** Trading.com has identifiable authorizations in major jurisdictions. However, traders should verify the exact account-holding company, financial product, and legal protections before depositing funds.

## Trading.com fees and spreads

Trading.com primarily uses commission-free, spread-based pricing.

For standard forex trades, the broker generally earns through the difference between the bid and ask prices.

### How competitive are Trading.com spreads?

The spreads vary by entity and instrument.

Its UK and EU account pages advertise a low average spread figure of 0.6 pips, while its Australian account page displays 0.8.

These published headline figures should not be treated as universal EUR/USD averages across every jurisdiction.

In [FXEmpire's independent US review](https://www.fxempire.com/brokers/tradingcomus), the researcher measured the following spreads on May 15, 2025:

| Instrument | London-session test | New York-session test |
|---|---:|---:|
| EUR/USD | 1.2 pips | 1.4 pips |
| GBP/JPY | 4.3 pips | 4.0 pips |

The reviewer described overall US forex trading costs as broadly average.

These are historical test observations, not current live quotes.

### Does Trading.com charge commissions?

The ordinary retail trading accounts advertise no separate standard forex dealing commission.

Trading costs are primarily incorporated into the spread.

However, rollover and other account-specific charges may apply.

The [official US pricing page](https://www.trading.com/us/forex-pricing) explains the broker's spread-based structure.

### Example: EUR/USD trading cost

Consider an illustrative one-standard-lot EUR/USD transaction.

Assume a spread of 1.3 pips and a pip value of approximately $10.

| Cost component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Example spread | 1.3 pips |
| Estimated spread cost | $13 |

| Illustrated transaction cost | $13 |

The spread is an illustrative midpoint between the two independent US observations.

It is not a guaranteed average or live quote.

Actual costs depend on market conditions, order size, and the relevant account.

### Does Trading.com offer a raw-spread account?

No conventional raw-spread commission account was identified in its reviewed 2026 retail lineup.

This can be a drawback for high-frequency forex traders who prefer tight institutional-style spreads with separate commissions.

### Is Trading.com a market maker?

Trading.com US states that it is the counterparty to customer forex transactions.

Its [official US execution and pricing disclosure](https://www.trading.com/us/forex-pricing) explains that trades are matched internally and exposures exceeding specified thresholds may be hedged with liquidity providers.

This execution model can involve potential conflicts of interest, which makes transparent pricing, execution policies, and effective regulatory oversight important.
